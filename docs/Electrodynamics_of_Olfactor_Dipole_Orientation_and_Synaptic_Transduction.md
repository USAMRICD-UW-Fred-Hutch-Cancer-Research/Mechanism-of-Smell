## **1\. Rotational Electrodynamics and Receptor Dipole Alignment**

When an odorant molecule enters the nasal cavity, it partitions from the gas phase into the lossy dielectric matrix of the high-hydration mucous layer, which acts as a primary conductive filter \[Gabriel, 1996; Venkatesh & Raghavan, 2004\]. Within this fluid boundary, the molecule’s permanent or induced **dipole moment vector (\$\\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_1\$)** dictates its long-range orientation and spinning kinetics as it approaches the positively charged amino acid pockets of an olfactory G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) matrix (\$\\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_2\$) \[Sartori & Lloyd, 2014; VirusTC, 2026\].

The total torque (\$\\boldsymbol{\\tau}\$) exerted by the receptor's localized electrostatic potential field (\$\\mathbf{E}\$) on the incoming spinning odorant is mathematically defined as:

\$\$\\boldsymbol{\\tau} \= \\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_1 \\times \\mathbf{E}\$\$

`[ Volatile Odorant Entry ]`   
           `│`  
           `▼`  
`[ Mucosal Dissolution Window ] ──► Establishes High-Permittivity Fluid Medium (εr = 62.2)`  
           `│`  
           `▼`  
`[ Rotational Electro-Torque ] ───► Odorant Vector (μ1) Aligns with GPCR Receptor Pocket (μ2)`  
           `│`  
           `▼`  
`[ Resonant Charge Release ] ────► Drops Energy State (V -> min) to Force Voltage Trigger (ΔV)`

As the spatial separation (\$r\$) closes, the interaction energy (\$V\$) between the odorant’s charge coordinates and the receptor's binding node operates inversely to the cube of the distance (\$1/r^3\$) \[Sartori & Lloyd, 2014\]:

\$\$V \= \\frac{1}{4\\pi\\varepsilon\_0\\varepsilon\_r} \\cdot \\frac{1}{r^3} \\left\[ \\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_1 \\cdot \\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_2 \- 3(\\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_1 \\cdot \\hat{\\mathbf{r}})(\\boldsymbol{\\mu}\_2 \\cdot \\hat{\\mathbf{r}}) \\right\]\$\$

The positively charged receptor site functions as an electron receiver \[VirusTC, 2026\]. As the odorant molecule spins to line up its complementary negatively charged surface plane with the receptor's charge density contour, the localized electrostatic field minimizing energy state (\$V \\rightarrow \\text{minimum}\$) acts as a mechanical latch. This convergence forces a localized charge release into the receptor’s transmembrane \$\\alpha\$-helices, triggering a structural conformational shift that activates the internal G-protein pathway (\$\\text{G}\_{\\text{olf}}\$) \[Sartori & Lloyd, 2014; VirusTC, 2026\].

## **3\. Electrochemical Transduction and Transmembrane Voltage Triggers**

Once the odorant dipole aligns and locks within the GPCR node, the biological system converts this precise chemical geometry into distinct electrical impulses along olfactory sensory neurons (OSNs) \[VirusTC, 2026\]. This transduction pathway operates as a series of cascade equations:

> 1. **Adenenyl Cyclase (ACIII) Activation:** The activated \$\\text{G}\_{\\text{olf}}\$ subunit upregulates adenylyl cyclase, converting intracellular ATP mass into cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP).  
> 2. **Ion Gradient Influx:** The sudden surge in local cAMP opens cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) ion channels, forcing a massive, rapid influx of external sodium (\$\\text{Na}^+\$) and calcium (\$\\text{Ca}^{2+}\$) vectors from the high-hydration mucosal background into the intracellular space.  
> 3. **The Chloride Outflux Amplification:** To amplify the signal, the entering \$\\text{Ca}^{2+}\$ opens calcium-activated chloride channels (ANO2), driving native chloride ions (\$\\text{Cl}^-\$) out of the cell dendrite.  
> 4. **Action Potential Propagation:** This parallel ion migration forces a sharp positive shift in the neuron’s intracellular charge density, altering the resting membrane potential to cross the depolarization threshold:  
>    \$\$\\Delta V\_{\\text{mem}} \= V\_{\\text{int}} \- V\_{\\text{ext}} \\ge \-40\\text{ mV}\$\$  
>    This voltage shift triggers local voltage-gated sodium channels, sending high-frequency electrical spikes traveling through the perforations of the low-permittivity cribriform plate (\$\\varepsilon\_r \= 11.40\$) and straight into the olfactory bulb glomeruli for real-time cortical tracking \[Gabriel, 1996; VirusTC, 2026\].
